## 4. VIDEO DESCRIPTION
Picture your entire world as thirty people. Your mother, your father, your siblings, your grandmother, your aunts, your uncles and every cousin you have ever met. Now find someone to have children with. That was the real situation for most of human prehistory, and it is a genuine survival problem, not a moral one.

This video explains what inbreeding actually does in plain terms, then shows the astonishing evidence that ancient humans solved it on purpose. At Sunghir in Russia, people buried side by side beneath thousands of mammoth-ivory beads turned out to be barely related when their genomes were sequenced. That is not what random pairing in a small band produces.

It covers the machinery they built: young adults leaving their birth group, bands gathering at good seasons to arrange partnerships, near-universal rules about who you may not marry, and an instinct that seems to switch off attraction to the people you grew up with. Then it contrasts all of that with Neanderthal genomes, which show close-relative parents, low diversity and small isolated groups. And it ends where the evidence actually leads: the network built to find unrelated partners is the same network that carried information, materials, language and technique across whole continents.
